# Impact Career Tutorials success-story research

Research date: 13 September 2026

This is a read-only evidence pass for the proposed success-stories page. The
important distinction is between a public result, an ICT-published story, an
independent report about ICT activity, and a marketing claim that still needs
owner documents. A result list proves that a person was recommended for a post;
it does not prove which coaching institute caused the result.

## What is safe to publish now

The strongest honest page can contain three clearly labelled groups:

1. **On the official APSC list:** Priyanka Saikia and Amitesh Rajkhowa, with
   post, serial number, result date, and a direct result link. The heading should
   say “Two names on the APSC CCE 2022 result,” not “Our top ranks.”
2. **Stories published by ICT:** Mithu Guwala and the ten student-review
   videos, each linked to the original ICT video. These are useful social proof,
   but they should not carry the same verified mark as the APSC list.
3. **People and institutions connected to ICT:** guest officers, the Lakhimpur
   Girls’ College collaboration, and the college result notice once its PDF is
   obtained. These demonstrate community presence and access.

## Owner verification request

Before the page claims that any named person studied at ICT or that ICT caused a
selection, ask the owner for:

- the student’s written permission to publish their name, face, result and
  story;
- the exact batch/course and dates attended;
- the official result, appointment order, or department roster;
- permission to quote the student or embed their video;
- the source PDF behind the Panigaon college notice;
- a list of any additional students shown on ICT result posters, with each name
  matched to an official result document.

## Claims to avoid

- “ICT produced two officers” based only on the APSC PDF;
- “rank 31” or “rank 46” when the document shows serial numbers in a post list;
- “100% success,” “highest selection rate,” “most selections,” or any total
  count without a dated denominator and source;
- treating a guest officer, a student review, or a workshop attendee as a
  successful ICT alumnus;
- copying result-poster claims that cannot be matched to an official result;
- publishing student names, faces, quotes, or WhatsApp screenshots without
  explicit permission.

## Recommended next step

Do not invent more success stories for launch. Use the verified two-person
section plus the clearly attributed ICT video section, then make the owner
approval checklist the next deliverable. The page becomes substantially more
persuasive when every card says exactly what kind of evidence it represents.
